Ask4NV learns novel-view generation jointly with 3D language understanding in one unified multimodal model. Spatial question answering and visual grounding supervise the generation transformer through joint self-attention, improving image fidelity and geometric consistency in and out of domain.

Learning to answer spatial questions makes a generative model render better novel views. Ask4NV is a framework that trains novel-view generation jointly with 3D language understanding, so that spatial question answering and grounding supervise the generation model.

Motivation

Novel-view generation methods rely on fine-grained geometric cues such as depth-based warping and cross-view correspondence. These cues capture scene structure, but not the high-level scene properties that also determine how a scene should look from a new viewpoint: object identities, attributes, and inter-object relationships. 3D language understanding learns exactly these properties, by answering questions about objects and their spatial relations. We therefore ask whether learning to answer spatial questions can supervise novel-view generation.

1. Does it improve novel-view generation?

If spatial supervision helps, it has to show first as better novel views, and not only on the training distribution. We compare against task-specific novel-view generators and a unified multimodal baseline, in and out of domain.

Finding 1Ask4NV improves novel-view image quality across multiple benchmarks.

Four source views, four target views, on DL3DV and ScanNet (in domain) and RE10K and ScanNet++ (out of domain), against CAT3D, Matrix3D, NVComposer, CAMEO, and Omni-View. PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS measure image fidelity; camera-pose error and MEt3R measure 3D consistency. Ask4NV has the best PSNR and LPIPS on all four datasets and the best pose accuracy, and the out-of-domain gains show that the improvement generalizes.

2. What does 3D language supervision improve inside the generation transformer?

The understanding objectives are losses on language: answering spatial questions and grounding objects. We ask whether they nonetheless change the spatial representations the generation transformer builds for its VAE latent tokens, measured by cross-view correspondence, which is never supervised during training.

Finding 23D language supervision improves cross-view correspondence in VAE representations.

Lance-FT is the same Lance checkpoint fine-tuned on novel-view generation only. In a two-view setting we take clean VAE tokens after attention at each layer, match every object token to its most similar token in the other view, and score with PCK@1/2/4. Layer 26 gives the strongest correspondence for both models, and Ask4NV outperforms Lance-FT on both datasets at every threshold. Correspondence in these VAE representations improves without any direct correspondence supervision: the benefit of 3D language understanding reaches the spatial representations used for generation.

3. Does joint training make generated views closer to the ground truth in a 3D-aware feature space?

We ask whether joint training also brings the generated views closer to the ground truth in a 3D-aware representation space: the feature space of an encoder trained for 3D language understanding, which encodes object identities, attributes, and spatial layout.

Finding 3Joint training improves similarity in a 3D-aware feature space.

The encoder is the SigLIP vision encoder fine-tuned for 3D language understanding inside Video-3D-LLM. We extract features of the generated and ground-truth views at the same target viewpoint and report the mean cosine similarity between spatially corresponding patches. Ask4NV is higher than the generation-only baseline on all four datasets, so joint training produces views that are more closely aligned with the ground truth in the feature space of a 3D language understanding encoder.
